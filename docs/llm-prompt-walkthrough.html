What the LLM reads, and is told, when it selects past episodes

What this page is for: seeing the LLM's whole briefing in one place. Every blue or purple box is the exact text that is sent or received. The grey column on the right explains each box in plain words and is not part of any message. The wording was decided on 2026-11-19. Until the agent is built, this page is where that wording lives; after that, it is kept in step with the real prompt.

The story in plain words

Every 15 minutes the agent looks at each asset. When Jev (the decision model) says an asset is worth it, the agent runs a full evaluation of that asset. Before Jev decides, it gets to read a few records of the agent's own earlier decisions and how they turned out. The LLM's only job is to choose which records. It does not trade.

Past episodeOne stored record: what the market looked like, what Jev decided, how it ended. LongThe account gains when the price rises. Buy $6,000 of a stock; it rises 1%; the account is up $60. ShortThe account gains when the price falls. On a $6,000 short, a 1% fall puts the account up $60 and a 1% rise puts it down $60. Entry priceThe price at which a trade was opened, say $100.00. Stop priceCaps what a trade can lose: code closes the trade if the price reaches it. A $6,000 long opened at $100.00 with a stop price of $99.40 loses about $36 at most. A tight stop price is close to the entry price (a small loss, easily reached); a wide one is far away. Candle patternA named shape in the last few price bars ("hammer", "bullish engulfing") that traders read as a hint. Track recordThe totals over the agent's closed trades: "42 trades, 17 wins, average win +$31, average loss -$18". Trading costsWhat trading itself takes from the account, such as $1 each time a stock is bought or sold. Every profit and loss on this page is after them. LookupOne use of a tool that reads memory. The LLM gets 6 per full evaluation. Giving its answer is not a lookup. TokenThe unit a model's reading is billed by, about three quarters of a word. Reading 1,000 tokens (roughly 750 words) costs about a hundredth of a cent on the small model planned.
