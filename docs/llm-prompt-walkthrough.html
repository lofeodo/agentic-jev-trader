The story in plain words

Every 15 minutes the agent looks at each asset. When Jev (the decision model) says an asset is worth it, the agent runs a full evaluation of that asset. Before Jev decides, it gets to read a few records of the agent's own earlier decisions and how they turned out. The LLM's only job is to choose which records. It does not trade. It does the same job in a second place. Once a trade is open, Jev answers a quick yes-or-no question about it every 15 minutes: is it worth reconsidering now? On a yes the agent runs a position review, and the LLM chooses which past position reviews Jev reads before it decides to hold, close, reduce, or add (tab 6).

Past episodeOne stored record: what the market looked like, what Jev decided, how it ended. PositionA holding that is open: $6,000 of a stock bought and not yet sold. Position reviewReconsidering an open position: hold it, close it, reduce it (sell part), or add to it (buy more). It runs only when the position screen, a quick yes-or-no question to Jev, says it is worth it. StandingHow a position stands at one moment: how long it has been open, its profit or loss so far, and how far away its stop price is. "Open 6 bars, +$18, stop price 0.9% away" is a standing. LongThe account gains when the price rises. Buy $6,000 of a stock; it rises 1%; the account is up $60. ShortThe account gains when the price falls. On a $6,000 short, a 1% fall puts the account up $60 and a 1% rise puts it down $60. Entry priceThe price at which a trade was opened, say $100.00. Stop priceCaps what a trade can lose: code closes the trade if the price reaches it. A $6,000 long opened at $100.00 with a stop price of $99.40 loses about $36 at most. A tight stop price is close to the entry price (a small loss, easily reached); a wide one is far away. Candle patternA named shape in the last few price bars ("hammer", "bullish engulfing") that traders read as a hint. Track recordThe totals over the agent's closed trades: "42 trades, 17 wins, average win +$31, average loss -$18". Trading costsWhat trading itself takes from the account, such as $1 each time a stock is bought or sold. Every profit and loss on this page is after them. LookupOne use of a tool that reads memory. The LLM gets 6 per full evaluation and 6 per position review. Giving its answer is not a lookup. TokenThe unit a model's reading is billed by, about three quarters of a word. Reading 1,000 tokens (roughly 750 words) costs about a hundredth of a cent on the small model planned.
