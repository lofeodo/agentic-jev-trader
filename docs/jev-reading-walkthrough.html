The story in plain words

Every 15 minutes the agent looks at each asset. Jev (the decision model) makes every trading decision. Jev cannot chat: it is sent a background (a page of text describing the moment) and one or more questions with fixed options, and it answers each question with a probability for every option. Code writes every word Jev reads.

ScreenThe quick question asked for an asset the account holds nothing in: is a full evaluation worth running now? Jev answers with a number from 0 to 1. Full evaluationWhat runs when the screen says yes. Jev picks a trade or no trade, then says how much money to put in. PositionA holding that is open: $6,000 of a stock bought and not yet sold. Once it is fully closed it is a closed trade. Position screenThe quick question asked every 15 minutes about an open position: is it worth a position review now? Jev answers with a number from 0 to 1. On a no, nothing more is asked and the position is held. Position reviewWhat runs when the position screen says yes. Jev reads past position reviews like this moment and decides: hold the position, close it, make it smaller (reduce), or make it bigger (add). StandingHow a position stands at one moment: how long it has been open, its profit or loss so far, and how far away its stop price is. LongThe account gains when the price rises. Buy $6,000 of a stock; it rises 1%; the account is up $60. ShortThe account gains when the price falls. On a $6,000 short, a 1% fall puts the account up $60 and a 1% rise puts it down $60. Stop priceCaps what a position can lose: code closes the position if the price reaches it. A $6,000 long opened at $100.00 with a stop price of $99.40 loses about $36 if it closes there. Typical moveHow far the price usually travels in one 15-minute bar. If recent bars each covered about $0.60, the typical move is $0.60. Stop prices sit 1, 2, or 3 typical moves away: tight, medium, or wide. Account valueWhat the account would be worth in cash if every open position were closed now. LoanMoney the account borrows when it buys more than its cash covers. An account with $7,000 of cash that buys $8,000 of a stock borrows $1,000. SizeHow much money goes into a trade. Jev chooses among five sizes, from 20% to 200% of account value ($2,000 to $20,000 on a $10,000 account). Trade risk limitA rule code enforces: no trade may lose more than 1% of account value ($100 of $10,000) if it closes at its stop price. Code makes a trade smaller until it fits. Candle patternA named shape in the last few price bars ("hammer", "bullish engulfing") that traders read as a hint. Track recordThe totals over the agent's closed trades: "42 trades, 17 wins, average win +$31, average loss -$18". Past episodeOne stored record of an earlier decision: what the market looked like, what Jev decided, how it ended. Trading costsWhat trading itself takes from the account, such as $1 each time a stock is bought or sold. TokenThe unit Jev's reading is billed by. For this kind of text Jev counts about one token per 2.4 characters. Reading 4,000 tokens costs Jev about a sixtieth of a cent.
