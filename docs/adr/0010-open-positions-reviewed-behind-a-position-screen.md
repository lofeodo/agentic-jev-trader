# An open position is reconsidered only when its position screen says yes

A position is a holding that is open: $6,000 of a stock bought and not yet sold. Until now Jev (the hosted decision model) was asked at every step (every 15 minutes) whether to hold it, close it, reduce it (sell part), or add to it (buy more), and re-read the past episodes (stored records of earlier decisions) it had read when it opened the position. Now a position is handled the way one is opened. At every step Jev answers a position screen, a cheap yes-or-no question: is this position worth reconsidering now? On a no, nothing more is asked: the position is held and its stop price (the price at which code closes it to cap the loss, for example where that $6,000 is down about $100) keeps guarding it. On a yes, a position review runs: the LLM (the language model whose only job is choosing which past episodes Jev reads) selects past episodes for the position as it stands now, and Jev reads them and decides to hold, close, reduce, or add. Each position review is stored as an episode. Episode search (looking up past decisions like the current one) returns, for an open position, past position reviews of positions in the same direction: a long (a bet that the price rises) is compared with past longs, a short (a bet that it falls) with past shorts. It no longer returns past decisions to open a position. We chose this because knowing when to close, add, or reduce is where memory is worth most, and the episodes read at entry were chosen for a moment that has passed.

One exception to the screen: at the last step of a stock's session (its trading hours within one day) from which an order is still carried out the same day, every open stock position gets a position review with no position screen first, called an end-of-session review. A stock held past the close can open the next morning far from where it ended: $6,000 of a stock that opens 3% lower loses $180, whatever its stop price. Carrying a position overnight is therefore always decided, never drifted into.

## Considered Options

- **Keep asking hold, close, reduce, or add at every step, with the entry memory** (the episodes read when the position was opened). No new kind of episode, but the memory read is about the moment of entry, and every extra ask is another chance to close a good position on a wobble in Jev's answer.
- **Call the LLM at every step for every position.** The freshest memory, at about seven times the LLM spending: about $18 against about $2.40 for one trading sim (one replay of the agent over a stretch of market history) of 30 days.
- **On a no, still ask the old every-step question.** A safety net, but a position could then be closed at a moment the screen called unremarkable, and decisions that move money would be made with two different memories.
- **Search past decisions to open a position** that look like the present moment ("would I open this now?"). The search as it was, but it finds positions that began like this, not positions that stood like this part-way through.
- **Rank the search by the position's standing** as well as by the chart. A standing is how a position stands at one moment: how long it has been open, its profit or loss so far, and how far away its stop price is ("open 6 bars, up $18, stop price 0.9% away"). The standing depends on the sim. So it cannot be scaled from the warm-up period (the three months before any sim trades, used to work out what is normal for each measurement, ADR 0004), and it cannot be part of the text that is turned into numbers for the search when a dataset is built. The standing is instead shown on each result line for the LLM to weigh.

## Consequences

- The agent without memory (the comparison that shows whether memory helps) and the build-up sim (the one-year sim in which Jev trades alone to fill the base memory, the starting memory every later sim begins with) run the same position screen and the same end-of-session review, with no LLM and no past episodes. The agents therefore still differ only by memory, and the base memory holds position reviews to search.
- Jev can close, reduce, or add only in a position review. Between reviews only code acts on a position: the stop price, a forced close (code closing every position when account value falls too far), and the sim's last step.
- A past position review records two outcomes whatever was decided: where the price went after 1 bar, after 4 bars, and by the end of the day, and how the position ended (why it closed, how many bars after the review, and its total profit or loss). It can be searched once both are known.
- The entry memory is still stored with the position and shown with it, but Jev does not read it again. This replaces the re-reading decided earlier the same day.
- Undoing this means running the build-up sim again, since the base memory's position reviews and its closed trades both come from this rule.
- Spending is planned on the same guess as before: the expensive path, where the LLM is called (a full evaluation before a position is opened, or a position review), runs on about 15% of all checks (a check is one cheap question to Jev about one asset at one step). End-of-session reviews add at most 4 position reviews per replayed day, about 1 cent a day for each agent with memory.
