# A stock sits out its partial-volume days

On some days the agent would trade a stock on wrong information: it would see a market that looks asleep when it was not, and might pass on a $2,000 trade it would otherwise take. The cause is volume (how much was traded in a bar, the 15-minute summary of trading; for example 1,000 shares), which the agent reads to judge how much a price move counts: a rise on heavy trading is taken more seriously than one on light trading. Stock bars come from Twelve Data's free plan, and on some days its bars for a stock carry only part of the market's trading, though their prices look sound. Either some bars arrive with a volume of zero (JPMorgan and Exxon on about ten days of April 2024), or the day's bars carry on average 10% or less of the usual volume (all four stocks on 25 March 2025, among others). The volume cannot be recovered: Twelve Data's 1-minute and 5-minute bars are empty at the same times, while its daily bar shows a normal day. So such a day is a partial-volume day, and every bar the stock has that day counts as a missing bar: the agent does not look at the stock, and the other assets carry on. A stock's day is a partial-volume day when, after bars outside its session (its trading hours that day) are dropped, either any of its bars has zero volume, or its average volume per bar is under a quarter of the median (the middle value) of that stock's previous 20 sessions. We chose this because no data is safer than data that is quietly wrong, and because it reuses the rules missing bars already have. With the same decision the first dataset's start moves from 3 January 2022 to 3 April 2023: 2022 holds thousands of zero-volume bars, and the build-up run (the year-long run that fills the memory) and the runs that are measured all fall between March 2024 and May 2025.

## Considered Options

- **Switch to a data provider with full-market volume.** Massive (formerly Polygon) documents volume from every US exchange for US$29 a month. Databento would probably be free with its new-account credit, but it does not supply adjusted prices (prices corrected for splits and dividends), so that correction would be ours to write. Sign-up from Canada was confirmed for neither, and a switch reopens the licence and access checks already done for Twelve Data. Too much for a flaw on under 2 days in 100.
- **Trade on prices, with volume marked unknown.** Keeps the day, but every measurement that uses volume would need an "unknown" case, and the feature vector (the list of numbers by which two situations are compared) would need a made-up value in its place.
- **Change nothing.** Zero-volume bars would stay missing one at a time, leaving half-empty days whose remaining bars still understate volume, and the days with no zero-volume bar would pass unnoticed.
- **Compare a day's volume with Twelve Data's own daily bar.** The 15-minute volumes usually add up to 75% to 90% of the daily figure, but on some ordinary days to only 30% to 55% (the third Friday of March, June, September and December, for example), so that share cannot tell a bad day from a good one. A stock is compared with its own earlier sessions instead.
- **Keep the 2022 start and raise the limit on missing bars.** Until now a build refused to publish a dataset if any asset was missing more than 0.5% of its bars, and with their 2022 bars JPMorgan and Exxon miss almost 4%, much of it as whole days without volume. Nothing needs those dates any more.

## Consequences

- Measured on the first dataset's range (3 April 2023 to 31 October 2026, 899 sessions), the rule removes 6 to 17 sessions per stock: SPY 8, Apple 6, JPMorgan 16, Exxon 17. The build-up run loses 11 sessions of JPMorgan and 12 of Exxon out of 249.
- The test period (the dates kept aside for the final measurement) is two 30-day stretches, one in which prices fell and one in which they rose. Each stock sits out one session in each, and JPMorgan two in the falling one. A `memory_on` run (the agent with its memory) and a `memory_off` run (the agent without) read the same bars, so the comparison between them stays fair.
- The volume test has a wide margin: every session it catches sits at 10% or less of usual, and the quietest session it lets through sits at 29%.
- Bars outside a stock's session have to be dropped first, using the exchange calendar (the published list of the days and hours the stock exchange is open). On a day the exchange closes early, at 1 pm New York time, Twelve Data still returns bars up to the usual close, with zero volume: 3 July 2023 has eleven or twelve of them per stock, and would otherwise count as a partial-volume day.
- The average is taken per bar, so that a session cut short by an early close compares fairly with full ones.
- The rule covers stocks only. Bitcoin's bars come from Binance, where a zero-volume bar means the exchange was down for those minutes, so those stay missing one bar at a time.
- As with any missing bar, a position (a holding in a stock) that is already open keeps its last known price through the day, and its stop price (the price at which code closes it to cap the loss) can only trigger once bars return.
- A build no longer checks one limit but two: at most 0.5% of an asset's bars missing for any other reason, and at most 3% of a stock's sessions as partial-volume days, about 7 a year. The quality report lists every partial-volume day.
- The previous 20 sessions are taken as the data provider supplied them, earlier partial-volume days included. A long run of partial-volume days with no zero-volume bar would therefore lower what counts as usual and could hide its own later days. None has been seen.
- The quarter, the 20 sessions and both limits are settings. Changing the rule or its two settings means building a new dataset, since a published dataset never changes, and then repeating the build-up run, about $10.
- ADR 0004 gives early April 2022 as the first dataset's first tradable step. With the new start it is 3 July 2023.
