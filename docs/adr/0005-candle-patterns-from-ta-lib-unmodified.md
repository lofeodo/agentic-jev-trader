# Candle patterns come from TA-Lib unmodified, all 61, on three kinds of bar

A candle pattern is a named shape of one to five bars that traders read as a hint about where the price goes next; a "hammer", for example, is a bar that fell from $100.20 to $99.20 and recovered to close at $100.30, read as a hint of a rise. The agent needs code that recognises these shapes, and TA-Lib is the standard library for it, with 61 pattern detectors. We decided to take every one of the 61 exactly as TA-Lib (pinned to version 0.8.3) defines it, with no adjustments and no detectors of our own, and to run the same detectors on three kinds of bar: the 15-minute bars the agent decides on, and the hourly bars and daily bars built from them, the way a day trader (someone who opens and closes trades within the same day) checks the bigger picture before reading the chart they trade on. We chose this because reliability comes first: a standard tool used as it comes leaves no settings of ours to defend, and the track record (the agent's own scorecard of its closed trades, such as "hammer: 18 trades, 5 wins, average loss of $12"), not our judgment, is what tests whether a pattern is worth anything.

## Considered Options

- **A hand-picked list of 23 well-known patterns** (the original brief's). Rejected: the list was arbitrary, and nothing made those patterns better than the rest of TA-Lib's.
- **Our own check that the price had been falling or rising before the shape.** The textbook says a hammer only counts after a fall, and TA-Lib does not check. Rejected: it needs our own detectors and several numbers we would have to pick. Jev is told separately whether the price has been rising or falling.
- **Loosening the 25 patterns that need the price to jump between two bars.** TA-Lib was written for daily bars, where each day opens away from the last close, such as a close at $100.00 and an open at $100.50. Inside a day a 15-minute bar opens where the last one closed, so these patterns rarely appear there, and six never appeared once in over four years. Rejected in favour of the daily bars, where they work as designed.
- **Dropping patterns for being very common or very rare.** Rejected: a rare pattern costs nothing, and how much a common one is worth is for the track record to show.
- **Choosing patterns by how they would have traded in the past.** Never: it fits the list to history and flatters every later result.

## Consequences

- Something is recognised on about four 15-minute bars in five, often several names at once, and some names are near-copies that land on the same bars (three kinds of doji, a bar that closes where it opened). All are listed; there are no tie-break rules.
- Jev reads three lists: 15-minute patterns from the pattern window, the last four steps, each with its age; patterns on the latest finished hourly bar; patterns on the latest finished daily bar. Only finished bars count, so a day's pattern is never seen before the day ends.
- The pattern window is counted in clock steps, not in the asset's own bars. This is a deliberate exception to ADR 0002, which counts look-back windows in the asset's own bars: at a stock's first bar of a session the window holds nothing from the day before, in keeping with the next rule.
- A 15-minute or hourly pattern never stretches across a missing bar or a stock's overnight close. A daily pattern runs across consecutive days with bars, since the overnight jump is what those patterns were designed around.
- The detectors are untouched; the lean is our label on what they report. A pattern's lean is bullish (a hint of a rise), bearish (a hint of a fall), or neutral (indecision). TA-Lib reports each recognised pattern with a plus or a minus, and for 54 detectors plus means bullish and minus means bearish. Seven detectors' signs do not match the pattern's meaning, so their lean is set by hand. Six describe an undecided bar and are neutral: doji, long-legged doji, rickshaw man, high wave, short line, and spinning top. Gravestone doji is bearish although TA-Lib reports it as plus.
- The track record and the pattern filter the LLM can apply to episode search use the 15-minute list only. A closed trade is filed under every name in the pattern window at its entry, whatever the name's age, or under "none". The hourly and daily names are background that Jev reads, and they also go into the situation text.
- Episodes and the track record carry these names, so changing the list or the TA-Lib version later means building a new dataset, and what was recorded under the old names no longer lines up with it.
